# Who is building Fronk?

I'm Federico Russo. I graduated in Electronic and Telecommunications
Engineering at the University of Bologna. My thesis, **TideMesh**, concerned
the emulation of flying ad hoc networks (FANETs) using containers.

I now study **Communications Engineering**, also at the University of Bologna.
My internal research internship and thesis project focus on **Networks for AI**.
Fronk comes from that interest in the meeting point between computation,
hardware and the network connecting the machines.

The question behind it is fairly ordinary: can the computers we already have
do something useful together that none of them can do alone? The test cluster
includes two different Apple Silicon laptops, an Intel iMac and a small Linux
Shuttle. It is a good way to discover which assumptions about a distributed
system survive contact with actual cables and old CPUs.

## First release, first stitches

This is Fronk's **first public version**, not a finished design. There will
likely be bugs, inconsistencies and cases I have not encountered. I am
publishing it so other people can try it, inspect the code and help improve it.
A clear bug report, a simpler paragraph or a result from an unfamiliar machine
is a useful contribution.

Fronkhensteen has made it off the laboratory table. Its manners still need work.

I would particularly like to see [your cluster and its result](SHARE.md): the
machines, the cables, the allocation and a completed generation. Please share
what failed as well as what worked. That is how this becomes useful beyond
the computers on my desk.
